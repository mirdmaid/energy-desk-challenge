Energy Desk Challenge

Start with $100,000. You're assigned one of nine energy stocks at random and trade it plus its options, one month at a time, over five years of real prices. You only see the past. You are scored on Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown and IRR against simply holding the stock.

    Rules and assumptions

    • Turns. Each turn is one month. Trades fill at that month's closing price. Press "Next month" to see what happened.
    • Stocks. Nine S&P 500 energy names across four parts of the industry: integrated majors (XOM, CVX), exploration and production (COP, EOG, DVN), oilfield services (SLB, HAL) and refining (VLO, PSX). By default each new game assigns you one at random, so you can't pick the stock you already know went up. Because stocks differ, the leaderboard's "Vs. hold" column shows how much your IRR beat or trailed simply holding your stock.
    • Prices. Month-end closes adjusted for dividends and splits, so holding the stock earns its total return. Built-in data runs from January 2021 through the last completed month and is refreshed once a month. Source: Digrin price history, spot-checked against other quote sites.
    • Options. Historical option quotes are not freely available, so calls and puts are priced with Black-Scholes using the stock's trailing 12-month realized volatility (floor 15%) and the rate you set. You buy at the ask and sell at the bid, a 4% spread around fair value, plus $0.65 per contract. Options settle in cash at intrinsic value on expiry.
    • Margin. Stock needs 50% initial margin, long or short. Long options are paid in full. Short calls covered by 100 shares each need nothing extra. Naked short options need the premium plus the greater of 20% of the stock value minus the out-of-the-money amount, or 10% of the stock value. If equity falls below 75% of the requirement at a month end, everything is liquidated.
    • Cash. Positive cash earns the rate you set; borrowed cash costs that rate plus 1%.
    • Sharpe ratio = mean monthly excess return over cash ÷ standard deviation of monthly returns × √12. Max drawdown = largest peak-to-trough fall in month-end equity. IRR = the annual rate that sets the value of the $100,000 deposit equal to the final equity; with no other deposits or withdrawals it equals the compound annual growth rate.
